Privacy Policy

Reply Buddy: AI Keyboard · Effective 23 September 2026

Reply Buddy helps you write replies to messages. This policy explains exactly what the app does with your information. It is written to describe the app as it actually behaves, not as a general template. Where the honest answer is less flattering than the reassuring one, the honest answer is here.

The short version. There are two ways to get a reply, and they send different things. The keyboard (and Reply to a screenshot in the app) reads your screenshot on your phone and sends only the words, with verification codes masked first; on this path the picture is never sent. The Chat tab in the app sends the picture itself, with whatever is on it and whatever you type, because that is how it reads the conversation — and it asks you first. Our server keeps none of it; our AI provider deletes it within 30 days unless a safety check flags it. Your chats are saved on your phone until you delete them. There is no account and we never ask for your name or email. No message content of any kind is sent anywhere until you switch AI replies on yourself, and you can switch them off again at any time.

2. There is no account, but there is an identifier

Reply Buddy does not ask you to register. It does not ask for your name, email address, phone number, address, contacts or date of birth (though a picture you send from Chat carries whatever is on it). When the app first needs to talk to our server it creates an anonymous identifier — a random string with nothing personal in it — so that your subscription status and your daily usage count can be recognised. That identifier is not linked to your identity.

Two further per-install values exist, and we would rather list them than let "there is no account" imply there is nothing:

  • A device key, created by Apple's App Attest on your device. We store its public half and a counter, so we can tell that requests come from a genuine copy of the app rather than from a script. The private half never leaves the device's secure hardware and we never see it.
  • A device token, a random string the keyboard presents instead of a signature, because a keyboard extension cannot attest itself; the app presents it too when it cannot sign. We store only a hash of it, and it expires after 90 days.

Neither identifies you. Both are tied to the installation, not to a person. Deleting the app removes both from your phone; our server's records of them stay until you ask us to delete them, because it cannot tell when the app is deleted (section 6).

3. Screenshots: two paths, and what each one sends

Reply Buddy works by reading a screenshot of a conversation, and there are two ways it does that. They treat the picture differently, so they are described separately.

The keyboard, and Reply to a screenshot: the picture stays on your phone

  • Reply Buddy reads only your single most recent screenshot. In the keyboard that happens while the keyboard is open — when you open it, when a new screenshot appears, and when you tap the capture button. In the app it happens while the app is open. Nothing is read when neither is on screen, and it does not browse, scan or index your photo library.
  • The text is extracted on your device, using Apple's built-in on-device text recognition.
  • On this path the image itself is never uploaded. It does not reach our servers or any third party. Only the recovered text is sent, and only after you have turned AI replies on.
  • The top of the screenshot is cropped away before it is read. We cut a fixed band from the top — the height of the status bar and the bar carrying the other person's name — so in normal use neither is read at all. This is a measurement, not a recogniser: on an unusual layout a line from that area could survive the crop. We would rather tell you that than claim a guarantee the code does not make.

Chat: the picture itself is sent

The Chat tab in the app works differently, and on purpose: you attach a picture of a conversation, say what you want to say, and the AI reads the picture itself.

  • You choose the picture. You attach it with the iOS photo picker, which runs outside the app and gives Reply Buddy only the picture you pick; it can be any picture, not only a screenshot. If you gave Reply Buddy full Photos access, Use latest screenshot attaches your single newest screenshot instead. To offer that button, the Chat list looks up when your newest screenshot was taken; it opens the picture only when you tap it.
  • The whole picture is sent, uncropped and unfiltered. That includes the status bar, the other person's name and profile photo at the top, and any codes, numbers, faces or photos on it. Nothing is masked. If a screenshot shows something you would not want to share, crop it or choose another before you send it.
  • It is made smaller first. It is redrawn at no more than 1,568 pixels on its long side and saved as a new JPEG, which removes anything stored inside the original file, such as its location, date and camera details.
  • It is read by our AI provider, Anthropic's Claude, not on your phone.
  • It is sent again with every change you ask for, together with the chat so far, because our server keeps nothing between requests.
  • A send you started keeps going for up to about thirty seconds if you switch to another app, so the reply is there when you come back. Nothing starts while the app is closed.
  • Only after you allow it. The first time you send a picture, Chat asks for your permission separately from the keyboard (section 5).

Setup asks for full Photos access, which the keyboard and Reply to a screenshot need to read your newest screenshot, and setup cannot be finished without it. If you later limit or turn off that access, they can no longer read screenshots, while typing and your saved replies continue to work. Chat's photo picker needs no permission, so Chat can still send a picture you choose.

5. Nothing goes anywhere until you agree, and you can take it back

The AI features are off until you explicitly turn them on inside the app. Until you do, no message content of any kind is transmitted. For the keyboard this is enforced in three separate places in the code rather than trusted to one.

Chat asks a second time. Turning on AI replies covers the words the keyboard reads, not a picture. So the first time you send a picture from Chat, it asks once more, separately: it tells you that the whole picture goes to Anthropic's Claude with what you type, that our server keeps none of it, that Anthropic deletes it within 30 days unless a safety check flags it, and that your chats stay on your phone. Nothing is sent unless you choose Allow, and the app refuses to send a picture without that answer, whatever screen the request comes from. Your answer is stored on your phone.

To withdraw consent, open Reply Buddy and turn AI replies off in Settings. From that moment nothing further is sent: the keyboard stops offering replies and goes back to being a keyboard, Chat stops sending, and Chat's own permission is withdrawn too — if you turn AI replies on again, Chat asks again before it next sends a picture. Turning it off does not delete what is already held: your chats stay on your phone until you delete them (section 6), and for what is held elsewhere, see section 16.

To be straight with you about what else happens: the app itself does talk to the network when you open it, before you have consented to anything, in order to create the anonymous identifier, check your subscription, and send the analytics and crash reporting described in section 9. None of that carries message content.

On your phone

Reply Buddy keeps a private container on your device, readable only by the app and its keyboard, so the keyboard has something to work with. It holds the most recent conversation that was read, the most recent screen capture, the reply you were last given, your saved writing samples, your settings, a record of whether Pro is active, and the device key and token from section 2.

The conversation and the capture are overwritten each time you capture a new one — the keyboard keeps no history and no earlier conversation. Deleting the app removes the container and everything in it.

Separately, the sign-in that carries the anonymous identifier from section 2 — the identifier and the tokens that renew it, no content — is kept in the iOS keychain, so the keyboard can use it too. iOS may keep keychain items after an app is deleted, so a reinstall can come back as the same identifier. If you ask us to delete the identifier (section 16), that sign-in stops working and a reinstall starts a new one.

Chat keeps a history, and it is yours to delete. Chats are saved in the app's own private storage — not the shared container, so the keyboard cannot read them. For each chat that means the picture as it was sent, what you typed, the replies and what the chat showed beside them (the messages it took each reply to answer, any note or refusal), an instruction still waiting to be sent, the mode and the dates. Separately, the app records when you allowed Chat to send pictures; deleting chats does not remove that record, and turning AI replies off does. Chats stay until you delete them: swipe a chat away, or use Delete all chats in the Chat list or in Settings, and they are removed from your phone at once. Deleting the app removes them too. They are excluded from iCloud and device backups, so they do not move to a new phone or come back from a restore, and they are written with iOS file protection so they are unreadable while your phone is locked. Deleting a chat does not remove the original screenshot from your Photos library, and it cannot reach the copy our AI provider holds (section 7).

7. The AI provider

Replies are generated by Anthropic's Claude models. We want to be exact here rather than reassuring, because the distinction matters:

  • Anthropic's commercial terms state that Anthropic may not train models on customer content from its services.
  • Anthropic's privacy documentation states that inputs and outputs are automatically deleted within 30 days of receipt or generation — with exceptions. Where a submission is flagged under Anthropic's usage policy, inputs and outputs may be kept for up to two years, and trust-and-safety classification scores for up to seven. Anthropic may also keep them for longer where the law requires it.

This applies to a picture sent from Chat exactly as it does to text: a picture is an input like any other. Each send is a new input — asking for a change, or continuing a chat later, sends the picture again, and that copy is deleted on its own schedule. Anthropic does not offer early deletion of individual requests, so deleting a chat on your phone does not delete Anthropic's copy, and we cannot ask for that on your behalf; it is deleted on Anthropic's schedule above.

So “we do not store your messages” is true of us, and thirty days is not zero. We will not claim your conversations vanish the instant they are used, because that would not be accurate.

9. Analytics and crash reports

The main app — not the keyboard — sends product analytics through PostHog, and crash and error reports, with a short record of each time the app is used, through Sentry. What is sent is event names and counts, such as app_opened, full_access_granted and whether a purchase completed, and, from Chat, chat_started, chat_reply, chat_refined and chat_deleted with how many chats were deleted — counts only, never what you typed or what a picture showed. Each is sent with the app version, the device model and operating-system version, the device's language and time zone, its screen size, whether it is on Wi-Fi or cellular, and the IP address the request came from, and each is tied to the anonymous identifier from section 2, so that we can see how one installation uses the app over time without knowing who you are. Sentry also receives short diagnostic messages we log ourselves when something goes wrong, such as a failure to reach our own server. On its own it keeps a short trail of what happened before a report and attaches it to any report it sends: the screens you opened and the buttons you tapped, by their fixed names and identifiers, never anything you typed or anything a screen showed; and the web addresses the app called recently — ours, RevenueCat's and PostHog's, some of which contain the anonymous identifier — with the method, the time, the response code and the size of each. When a server answers with a failure (an HTTP 5xx response), it reports that as an error of its own. It also records when each session of using the app starts and ends, so that crash rates can be measured. It never records what a request or a response contained.

No message content, no picture, no reply text and no draft is ever included, and the keyboard extension contains no analytics software at all. Counts of how the keyboard was used — how many times it appeared, how much memory it used — are written to the shared container and sent by the app, as numbers with no text in them.

13. Where your information comes from

Three sources, and no others: from you (the screenshot you choose to capture, the picture you choose to send from Chat and what you type there, your draft, your writing samples and your settings); from your device (the App Attest key; details of the device and the app, such as the device model, the operating-system and app versions, language, time zone, screen size and whether it is on Wi-Fi or cellular, as described in section 9; and the IP address and the app's standard user-agent string that any request carries); and from Apple and RevenueCat (whether a subscription is active).

14. Apple's privacy label

In the App Store, Reply Buddy declares that User Content — Photos or Videos, Emails or Text Messages, and Other User Content — is collected and not linked to your identity: the picture you send from Chat, the conversation read from a screenshot and your writing samples, and what you type. We declare it because Apple defines collection as transmitting data off the device and storing it in readable form for longer than it takes to service the request — and our AI provider's thirty-day deletion window is longer than that. We would rather over-declare than describe this inaccurately.

16. Your rights

Because there is no account, most of your data is either on your device, or is an anonymous counter and the records listed in section 6. You can delete your chats in the app at any time (section 6), and the app's files on your phone by deleting the app; the sign-in in the iOS keychain may outlive it (section 6). Neither reaches the copy our AI provider holds: Anthropic deletes it within 30 days of each send, or keeps it longer — up to two years if its safety systems flag it, or as long as the law requires — and it does not delete early on request (section 7). To stop anything further being sent, turn AI replies off (section 5).

To ask what is held against your anonymous identifier, or to have it deleted, email tiko23miko23@gmail.com. We will not ask you to create an account to make the request. Because the identifier is anonymous, you will need to send it to us so we can find the right record — the app shows it under Settings → About. Depending on where you live you may have rights of access, correction, deletion and portability, the right to object to or restrict processing, the right not to be discriminated against for exercising them, and the right to complain to your data protection authority.

17. A word about the other person

A screenshot of a conversation contains someone else's words as well as your own — and a picture sent from Chat also contains their name, their profile photo and anything else on the screen, such as photos they sent you. You decide what to capture and send, and you are responsible for it. Please think about whether the other person would object, do not send pictures of other people that they would not expect to be shared, and do not use Reply Buddy on conversations you are not meant to be reading.

18. Data transfers and security

Our servers and our AI provider operate in the United States, so if you use the app elsewhere your message text, and from Chat the picture you send, are processed there. All transmission is encrypted in transit (HTTPS/TLS). The conversation stored on your device, and your chats, are written with iOS file protection so they are unreadable while your phone is locked. No transmission or storage is ever completely secure, and we cannot promise absolute security.
