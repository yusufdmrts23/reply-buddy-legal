Privacy Policy

Reply Buddy: AI Keyboard · Effective 21 September 2026

Reply Buddy helps you write replies to messages. This policy explains exactly what the app does with your information. It is written to describe the app as it actually behaves, not as a general template. Where the honest answer is less flattering than the reassuring one, the honest answer is here.

The short version. The screenshot image never leaves your phone. The words read from it are sent to our server and on to an AI service to write your reply, with verification codes masked first, and neither we nor that service keeps them in a permanent record. There is no account and we never see your name or email. No message content of any kind is sent anywhere until you switch AI replies on yourself, and you can switch them off again at any time.

1. Who we are

Reply Buddy is published by Yusuf Demirtaş, an individual developer, who is the data controller for the purposes of this policy. You can reach us at tiko23miko23@gmail.com.

2. There is no account, but there is an identifier

Reply Buddy does not ask you to register. It does not collect your name, email address, phone number, address, contacts or date of birth. When the app first needs to talk to our server it creates an anonymous identifier — a random string with nothing personal in it — so that your subscription status and your daily usage count can be recognised. That identifier is not linked to your identity.

Two further per-install values exist, and we would rather list them than let "there is no account" imply there is nothing:

  • A device key, created by Apple's App Attest on your device. We store its public half and a counter, so we can tell that requests come from a genuine copy of the app rather than from a script. The private half never leaves the device's secure hardware and we never see it.
  • A device token, a random string the keyboard presents instead of a signature because a keyboard extension cannot attest itself. We store only a hash of it, and it expires after 90 days.

Neither identifies you. Both are tied to the installation, not to a person, and both disappear when you delete the app.

3. Screenshots stay on your phone

Reply Buddy works by reading a screenshot of a conversation. To be precise about what that involves:

  • Reply Buddy reads only your single most recent screenshot. In the keyboard that happens while the keyboard is open — when you open it, when a new screenshot appears, and when you tap the capture button. In the app it happens while the app is open. Nothing is read when neither is on screen, and it does not browse, scan or index your photo library.
  • The text is extracted on your device, using Apple's built-in on-device text recognition.
  • The image itself is never uploaded. It does not reach our servers or any third party. Only the recovered text is sent, and only after you have turned AI replies on.
  • The top of the screenshot is cropped away before it is read. We cut a fixed band from the top — the height of the status bar and the bar carrying the other person's name — so in normal use neither is read at all. This is a measurement, not a recogniser: on an unusual layout a line from that area could survive the crop. We would rather tell you that than claim a guarantee the code does not make.

This requires Photos permission, which iOS asks for during setup. If you decline, the app cannot read screenshots; typing and your saved replies continue to work.

4. What is sent when you ask for replies

When you request a reply, the following is sent to our server and then, apart from the last two rows, on to our AI provider:

SentDetail
Screen textEvery line of text read from the screenshot below the cropped top band, with where each line sat on the screen and the colour behind it, so the AI can tell the messages from the buttons and who said what. Device evidenceThe App Attest signature, or the keyboard's device token, described in section 2. Sent with every request, checked by our server, and never passed on to the AI provider

Text read from a screenshot is filtered before it is sent. A line that looks machine-generated — a verification code, a security notice, a “do not reply” message — is dropped entirely, and a run of digits that a nearby word marks as a code is replaced with ••••. A screenshot with nothing left after that is not sent at all. Your draft and your saved writing samples are your own words, typed by you, and are sent exactly as you wrote them.

We never send: the screenshot image, your contacts, the name of the app you are replying in, your location, your identity, or anything you type on the keyboard outside of a reply request.

About other people's names. The cropped band removes the contact name at the top of a chat. It does not remove names that appear inside the conversation itself — in a group chat the sender labels are ordinary lines on the screen and are read like any other line. If you capture a group conversation, the participants' display names are part of the text that is sent.

5. Nothing goes anywhere until you agree, and you can take it back

The AI features are off until you explicitly turn them on inside the app. Until you do, no message content of any kind is transmitted. This is enforced in three separate places in the code rather than trusted to one.

To withdraw consent, open Reply Buddy and turn AI replies off in Settings. From that moment nothing further is sent: the keyboard stops offering replies and goes back to being a keyboard. Turning it off does not delete what is already held — to ask for that, see section 16.

To be straight with you about what else happens: the app itself does talk to the network when you open it, before you have consented to anything, in order to create the anonymous identifier, check your subscription, and send the analytics and crash reporting described in section 9. None of that carries message content.

6. What we keep, and for how long

On our servers

We do not store your message content. Conversation text, drafts and generated replies are not written to our database and are not written to our logs. They exist only for the seconds it takes to produce your reply. Everything we do keep is listed here:

Each of those rows is tied to the anonymous identifier and none of them contains anything you wrote. Every request to our server, like every request to any web service, arrives with your IP address attached; our own logs do not record it, and our hosting provider's platform logs may retain it briefly for operational purposes.

On your phone

Reply Buddy keeps a private container on your device, readable only by the app and its keyboard, so the keyboard has something to work with. It holds the most recent conversation that was read, the most recent screen capture, the reply you were last given, your saved writing samples, your settings, a record of whether Pro is active, and the device key and token from section 2.

The conversation and the capture are overwritten each time you capture a new one — there is no history and no earlier conversation is kept. Deleting the app removes the container and everything in it.

7. The AI provider

Replies are generated by Anthropic's Claude models. We want to be exact here rather than reassuring, because the distinction matters:

  • Anthropic's commercial terms state that Anthropic may not train models on customer content from its services.
  • Anthropic's privacy documentation states that inputs and outputs are automatically deleted within 30 days of receipt or generation — with exceptions. Where a submission is flagged under Anthropic's usage policy, inputs and outputs may be kept for up to two years, and trust-and-safety classification scores for up to seven.

So “we do not store your messages” is true of us, and thirty days is not zero. We will not claim your conversations vanish the instant they are used, because that would not be accurate.

9. Analytics and crash reports

The main app — not the keyboard — sends product analytics through PostHog and crash and error reports through Sentry. What is sent is event names and counts, such as app_opened, full_access_granted and whether a purchase completed, along with the app version, the device model and operating-system version, and the IP address the request came from. Sentry also receives short diagnostic messages we log ourselves when something goes wrong, such as a failure to reach our own server.

No message content, no reply text and no draft is ever included, and the keyboard extension contains no analytics software at all. Counts of how the keyboard was used — how many times it appeared, how much memory it used — are written to the shared container and sent by the app, as numbers with no text in them.

10. Subscriptions

Purchases are handled by Apple and managed through RevenueCat, which records what you bought and whether it is active. We never see or receive your payment details — Apple processes payment and does not share your card information with us.

12. We do not sell or share your personal information

We do not sell your personal information, and we have not done so. We do not share it for cross-context behavioural advertising, and we have not done so. There is no advertising in Reply Buddy and no advertising network receives anything from it. We do not use your message content to build a profile of you, to infer anything about you, or for any purpose other than producing the reply you asked for.

13. Where your information comes from

Three sources, and no others: from you (the screenshot you choose to capture, your draft, your writing samples and your settings); from your device (the App Attest key, the app version and device model, and the IP address any request carries); and from Apple and RevenueCat (whether a subscription is active).

14. Apple's privacy label

In the App Store, Reply Buddy declares that User Content is collected and not linked to your identity. We declare it because Apple defines collection as transmitting data off the device and storing it in readable form for longer than it takes to service the request — and our AI provider's thirty-day deletion window is longer than that. We would rather over-declare than describe this inaccurately.

15. Children

Reply Buddy is not directed at children. It is rated for users aged 17 and over and should not be used by anyone under 13, or under the minimum age of digital consent where you live. We do not knowingly collect information from a child. If you believe a child has used the app and you would like what is held against that installation removed, write to us and we will remove it.

16. Your rights

Because there is no account, most of your data is either on your device or is an anonymous counter. You can delete everything held on your device by deleting the app.

To ask what is held against your anonymous identifier, or to have it deleted, email tiko23miko23@gmail.com. We will not ask you to create an account to make the request. Because the identifier is anonymous, you will need to send it to us so we can find the right record — the app shows it under Settings → About. Depending on where you live you may have rights of access, correction, deletion and portability, the right to object to or restrict processing, the right not to be discriminated against for exercising them, and the right to complain to your data protection authority.

17. A word about the other person

A screenshot of a conversation contains someone else's words as well as your own. You decide what to capture, and you are responsible for what you choose to capture and send. Please think about whether the other person would object, and do not use Reply Buddy on conversations you are not meant to be reading.

18. Data transfers and security

Our servers and our AI provider operate in the United States, so if you use the app elsewhere your message text is processed there. All transmission is encrypted in transit (HTTPS/TLS). The conversation stored on your device is written with iOS file protection so it is unreadable while your phone is locked. No transmission or storage is ever completely secure, and we cannot promise absolute security.
