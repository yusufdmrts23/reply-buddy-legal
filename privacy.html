Privacy Policy

Reply Buddy: AI Keyboard · Effective 24 September 2026

Reply Buddy helps you write replies to messages. The replies are written by AI — Anthropic's Claude — not by a person. This policy explains exactly what the app does with your information. It is written to describe the app as it actually behaves, not as a general template. Where the honest answer is less flattering than the reassuring one, the honest answer is here.

The short version. There are two ways to get a reply, and they send different things. The keyboard (and Reply to a screenshot in the app) reads your screenshot on your phone and sends only the words, with verification codes masked first; on this path the picture is never sent. The Chat tab in the app sends the picture itself, with whatever is on it and whatever you type, because that is how it reads the conversation — and it asks you first. Our server keeps none of it; our AI provider deletes it within 30 days unless a safety check flags it. Your chats are saved on your phone until you delete them. There is no account and we never ask for your name or email. No message content of any kind is sent anywhere until you switch AI replies on yourself, and you can switch them off again at any time. You can delete your data from Settings; section 16 says exactly what that reaches. Reply Buddy is for adults: you must be 18 or older.

2. There is no account, but there is an identifier

Reply Buddy does not ask you to register. It does not ask for your name, email address, phone number, address, contacts or date of birth (though a picture you send from Chat carries whatever is on it). When the app first needs to talk to our server it creates an anonymous identifier — a random string with no name or contact details in it — so that your subscription status and your daily usage count can be recognised. It is still a persistent identifier: the app ties your analytics, crash reports and purchase record to it (sections 8 and 9), and the privacy laws of several states treat an identifier like this, and everything tied to it, as personal information. So do we.

A few further per-install values exist, and we would rather list them than let "there is no account" imply there is nothing:

  • A device key, created by Apple's App Attest on your device. We store its public half and a counter, so we can tell that requests come from a genuine copy of the app rather than from a script. The private half never leaves the device's secure hardware and we never see it.
  • A device token, a random string the keyboard presents instead of a signature, because a keyboard extension cannot attest itself; the app presents it too when it cannot sign. We store only a hash of it, and it stops working after 90 days.
  • Our analytics provider, PostHog, and our crash reporter, Sentry, each create their own random identifier for the installation, alongside ours.

None of these contains your name or contact details. Deleting the app removes them from your phone; our server's records stay until you delete them from Settings or ask us to (section 16), because our server cannot tell when the app is deleted.

3. Screenshots: two paths, and what each one sends

Reply Buddy works by reading a screenshot of a conversation, and there are two ways it does that. They treat the picture differently, so they are described separately.

The keyboard, and Reply to a screenshot: the picture stays on your phone

  • Reply Buddy reads only your single most recent screenshot. In the keyboard that happens while the keyboard is open — when you open it, when a new screenshot appears, and when you tap the capture button. In the app it happens while the app is open. Nothing is read when neither is on screen, and it does not browse, scan or index your photo library.
  • The text is extracted on your device, using Apple's built-in on-device text recognition.
  • On this path the image itself is never uploaded. It does not reach our servers or any third party. Only the recovered text is sent, and only after you have turned AI replies on.
  • The top of the screenshot is cropped away before it is read. We cut a fixed band from the top — the height of the status bar and the bar carrying the other person's name — so in normal use neither is read at all. This is a measurement, not a recogniser: on an unusual layout a line from that area could survive the crop. We would rather tell you that than claim a guarantee the code does not make.

Chat: the picture itself is sent

The Chat tab in the app works differently, and on purpose: you attach a picture of a conversation, say what you want to say, and the AI reads the picture itself.

  • You choose the picture. You attach it with the iOS photo picker, which runs outside the app and gives Reply Buddy only the picture you pick; it can be any picture, not only a screenshot. If you gave Reply Buddy full Photos access, Use latest screenshot attaches your single newest screenshot instead. To offer that button, the Chat list looks up when your newest screenshot was taken; it opens the picture only when you tap it.
  • The whole picture is sent, uncropped and unfiltered. That includes the status bar, the other person's name and profile photo at the top, and any codes, numbers, faces or photos on it. Nothing is masked. If a screenshot shows something you would not want to share, crop it or choose another before you send it.
  • It is made smaller first. It is redrawn at no more than 1,568 pixels on its long side and saved as a new JPEG, which removes anything stored inside the original file, such as its location, date and camera details.
  • It is read by our AI provider, Anthropic's Claude, not on your phone.
  • It is sent again with every change you ask for, together with the chat so far, because our server keeps nothing between requests.
  • A send you started keeps going for up to about thirty seconds if you switch to another app, so the reply is there when you come back. Nothing starts while the app is closed.
  • Only after you allow it. The first time you send a picture, Chat asks for your permission separately from the keyboard (section 5).

Setup asks for full Photos access, which the keyboard and Reply to a screenshot need to read your newest screenshot. You can continue without it, or with only some photos: they then cannot read new screenshots, while typing, your saved replies and Chat keep working — Chat's photo picker needs no permission.

Safety checks, in the same request

While it writes a reply, the AI also answers a few questions about what it was sent, so that a reply can be held back or changed when it would put someone at risk: whether the conversation involves someone thinking about harming themselves (so that the app never refuses to help then); in Chat, whether someone is asking for an intimate picture and what your instruction asks for (so that a small set of harmful requests is refused); and whether someone in a romantic conversation has said they are under 18 (so that no reply is written for it). These answers are processed automatically, only for the length of the request. Our server acts on them and does not store them. Its log line records only whether a reply was refused or held back (section 6) — never the answers themselves, which question led to a refusal, or your identifier — and none of the answers is used for anything but that reply. Our AI provider keeps them with the rest of the request, on the schedule in section 7.

Sensitive information in what you send

A conversation can contain sensitive information — something about a person's health, sex life or sexual orientation, religion, finances or immigration status, a crime they suffered, or the fact that they are a child. Several US states give that information extra protection, and California counts the contents of messages you send or receive (we are not their intended recipient) as sensitive personal information. Reply Buddy does not ask for any of it and has no use for it beyond writing the reply you asked for and keeping that reply safe (above). We do not sell it, share it for advertising, or build a profile from it. The only conclusions drawn from it are the safety checks above: our server uses them for that one reply and does not store them, and our AI provider keeps them with the rest of the request (section 7). You agree to this processing when you turn on AI replies and, for pictures, when you allow Chat; you can withdraw that agreement at any time by turning AI replies off (section 5). If a conversation you send mentions health, our Consumer Health Data Privacy Policy applies too.

5. Nothing goes anywhere until you agree, and you can take it back

The AI features are off until you explicitly turn them on inside the app. Until you do, no message content of any kind is transmitted. You can finish setup without turning them on. For the keyboard this is enforced in three separate places in the code rather than trusted to one.

Chat asks a second time. Turning on AI replies covers the words the keyboard reads, not a picture. So the first time you send a picture from Chat, it asks once more, separately: it tells you that the whole picture goes to Anthropic's Claude, the AI that writes the reply, with what you type and any writing samples you saved; that our server keeps none of it; that Anthropic deletes it within 30 days unless a safety check flags it; and that your chats stay on your phone. Nothing is sent unless you choose Allow, and the app refuses to send a picture without that answer, whatever screen the request comes from. Your answer is stored on your phone.

To withdraw consent, open Reply Buddy and turn AI replies off in Settings. From that moment nothing further is sent: the keyboard stops offering replies and goes back to being a keyboard, Reply to a screenshot and Chat stop sending, and Chat's own permission is withdrawn too — if you turn AI replies on again, Chat asks again before it next sends a picture. Turning it off does not delete what is already held: your chats stay on your phone until you delete them (section 6), and for what is held elsewhere, see section 16.

To be straight with you about what else happens: the app itself does talk to the network when you open it, before you have consented to anything, in order to create the anonymous identifier, check your subscription, and — unless you turn Share usage data off in Settings — send the analytics and crash reporting described in section 9. None of that carries message content.

7. The AI provider

Replies are generated by Anthropic's Claude models. We want to be exact here rather than reassuring, because the distinction matters:

  • We do not collect, use or sell personal data to train large language models, or any other AI model, and Anthropic's commercial terms state that Anthropic may not train models on customer content from its services.
  • Anthropic's privacy documentation states that inputs and outputs are automatically deleted within 30 days of receipt or generation — with exceptions. Where a submission is flagged under Anthropic's usage policy, inputs and outputs may be kept for up to two years, and trust-and-safety classification scores for up to seven. Anthropic may also keep them for longer where the law requires it.
  • Anthropic states that it checks images sent to its services against a database of known child sexual abuse material and reports matches to the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children.
  • Anthropic receives no identifier from us: nothing it is sent says which installation it came from.

This applies to a picture sent from Chat exactly as it does to text: a picture is an input like any other. Each send is a new input — asking for a change, or continuing a chat later, sends the picture again, and that copy is deleted on its own schedule. Anthropic does not offer early deletion of individual requests, so deleting a chat on your phone, or using Delete my data, does not delete Anthropic's copy, and we cannot ask for that on your behalf; it is deleted on Anthropic's schedule above.

So “we do not store your messages” is true of us, and thirty days is not zero. We will not claim your conversations vanish the instant they are used, because that would not be accurate.

9. Analytics and crash reports

The main app — not the keyboard — sends product analytics through PostHog, and crash and error reports, with a short record of each time the app is used, through Sentry. You can turn both off with Share usage data in Settings; they are then not started at all.

PostHog receives event names and counts, such as app_opened, full_access_granted and whether a purchase completed; from Chat, chat_started, chat_reply, chat_refined and chat_deleted with how many chats were deleted — counts only, never what you typed or what a picture showed; and, on its own, when the app is installed, updated, opened and sent to the background. Each event is sent with the app's name, version and build, the device's make, model and type, its operating-system version, language, time zone and screen size, whether it is on Wi-Fi or cellular, and the IP address the request came from, and each is tied to the anonymous identifier from section 2, so that we can see how one installation uses the app over time without knowing who you are. We tell PostHog not to work out a location from the IP address.

Sentry receives crash and error reports: short diagnostic messages we log ourselves when something goes wrong, such as a failure to reach our own server, and what the app and device were doing — the app version, the device model and architecture, its operating-system version, language, time zone and calendar settings, screen size, processor count, memory, thermal state, battery level and charging state, orientation and whether it appears to be jailbroken, and when the app was started. On its own it keeps a short trail of what happened before a report and attaches it to any report it sends: the screens you opened and the buttons you tapped, by their fixed names and identifiers, never anything you typed or anything a screen showed; and the web addresses the app called recently — ours, RevenueCat's and PostHog's, some of which contain the anonymous identifier — with the method, the time, the response code and the size of each. When a server answers with a failure (an HTTP 5xx response), it reports that as an error of its own. It also records when each session of using the app starts and ends, so that crash rates can be measured. Reports are tied to the anonymous identifier, and Sentry may work out an approximate location (such as country, region and city) from the IP address a report came from and keep it with the report. We remove the device identifier Sentry would otherwise add. It never records what a request or a response contained.

No message content, no picture, no reply text and no draft is ever included, and the keyboard extension contains no analytics software at all. Counts of how the keyboard was used — how many times it appeared, how much memory it used — are written to the shared container and sent by the app, as numbers with no text in them.

15. Children and teenagers

Reply Buddy is for adults. You must be 18 or older to use it, and it is not directed at children or teenagers. We do not knowingly collect personal information from anyone under 18.

Where the law requires an age check — for example for new Apple Accounts in Texas — and your iPhone runs iOS 26.2 or later, where Apple's age-range feature can tell an app that the law applies, the app asks Apple, through that feature, whether the account belongs to someone 18 or older. It does not open for anyone Apple reports as under 18, nor, where the law requires you to share your age range, until you do; if the check cannot finish, or Apple's answer does not show whether you are 18, it does not open and offers to try again. 16. Your rights

Delete my data. Settings → About → Delete my data deletes your chats, your writing samples and the last conversation and reply the keyboard kept, on this phone; deletes the anonymous identifier and everything our database holds for it (the table in section 6); and asks our analytics provider, PostHog, to delete the analytics tied to it, and our subscription provider, RevenueCat, to delete the purchase record it holds for it. 17. A word about the other person

A screenshot of a conversation contains someone else's words as well as your own — and a picture sent from Chat also contains their name, their profile photo and anything else on the screen, such as photos they sent you. You decide what to capture and send, and you are responsible for it.
